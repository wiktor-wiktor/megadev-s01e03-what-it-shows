What the desk should act on

The original incident says this is one known parser issue. Its rendered table and the exported report data show two causes. Keep a ticket for each:

  • Shared month-first parser: day-first input is interpreted month first. Harbour Dental’s R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July.
  • booking-v3 format hint: the form prints MM/DD/YYYY for day-first tenants but interprets input in their local convention. Copper Studio’s R-41230 describes 6 January becoming 1 June. Correct the hint and input agreement separately from the shared parser.

Arrival is different from merging

The received series starts before the bulk merge into the incident. A merge spike records desk activity; it does not mean the reports first arrived then. Later reports continue to arrive, so “known” and “fix queued” are not evidence of a resolved incident.

Match causes before merging

The old triage rule and accepted decision merge by subject alone. Their review dates are stale. The interactive examples below demonstrate that identical subjects can describe different causes, and different subjects can share a cause.
